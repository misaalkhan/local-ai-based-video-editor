# Local Studio

A local AI video editor and planner for reels and creator content. Drop in footage, describe the edit, tweak on a timeline, export. A planner page keeps your footage library, your goals and a content plan, and starts the edits for you. Everything runs on this Mac; only chat requests go to Claude.

## Start

    npm run setup      # one-time: ffmpeg, whisper.cpp, the speech and alignment models, fonts, music, sounds
    npm start          # builds the interface and serves it at http://localhost:4747

Or double-click `Local Studio.command` in Finder: it starts the app and opens it in your browser. Close that Terminal window to stop it.

Requirements: macOS on Apple Silicon, [Homebrew](https://brew.sh), Node 23.6 or newer, and the `claude` command signed in to your Claude account (the assistants run through it, so their work counts against your Claude plan).

## How it works

**Home.** Drop clips (or pick a whole folder with "Choose files on this Mac", which uses the files where they are), type what you want, choose a format, press Start. The project opens, the clips are prepared (preview copy, word-timed transcript, face tracking) and your request runs. "Start with an empty project" skips the request.

**The editor.** Left: Media, Music, Sounds, Memes, Captions, Text, Overlays, Effects, Transitions and Edit. Middle: the preview (Fit or zoom in). Right: the Assistant and the Transcript. Bottom: the timeline.

- Assistant: describe the edit ("make this a tight 40-second reel with a hook"). It reads the transcript, cuts, adds captions and graphics, then looks at rendered frames of its own work and fixes what is off. Every request can be undone from its message. Stop ends a run.
- Transcript: select words and press delete to cut them from the video; cut words stay struck through and can be restored. Fix a misheard word, emphasise a word in the captions, move a sentence earlier or to the start, and choose between repeated takes. When more than one person talks, the transcript shows who (S1, S2) at each change of speaker.
- Timeline: drag, trim, split (S), duplicate (⌘D), delete, undo (⌘Z). Drag clips, text styles, overlays and sound effects from the side panels onto it. Clean up: remove silences, remove fillers, add captions, cut clips to the beat of the music.
- Captions come from the transcript and follow every cut. Fourteen styles (pop, outline, one word at a time, karaoke, highlight, underline, neon glow, glass, typewriter, band, retro, comic, clean, boxed), any of the bundled fonts, and style recipes (a whole look: captions, fonts, colour, pacing, music level) that you can save from a project and apply to the next.
- Text: sixteen styles (hook, title, meme, comic, sticker, neon, handwritten, marker, quote, glitch, gradient, typewriter and more) with entrances (pop, bounce, zoom, typewriter, slides).
- Effects: punch-ins, slow push-ins, shake, framing (follow the face, fill or show the whole picture with a blurred background), nineteen colour looks plus warmth, vignette, sepia, film grain, cinema bars and mirror. Transitions: fade, dip, flash, slides, whip pan, zoom, blur, glitch.
- Layers: blend modes (screen, multiply, overlay, add and more), rectangle and ellipse masks with feather, corner radius and invert, and green-screen keying with colour pick from the corners, similarity, smoothness and spill removal, all on picture-in-picture clips.
- Keyframes: motion on any clip's picture (push in, pull out, pan, tilt, Ken Burns drift, or your own keyframes of zoom and focus) and placement keyframes on picture-in-picture clips and graphics (fly in from a side, grow in, or any position, size, rotation and opacity over time), with easing per keyframe. Keyframes show as diamonds on the timeline; the framing sliders write to the keyframe at the playhead.
- Speed: plain speed, speed ramps (presets: slow moment, hero, ramp in, ramp out, burst; or your own points, each holding a speed and changing evenly to the next), reverse, freeze frames (hold any frame for a chosen time, framed and graded like the clip), and smooth slow motion on export with frame blending or optical flow. The preview plays ramps live and steps through reversed clips; the export reads the source at its own frame rate and plays ramps, reverse and audio tempo exactly.
- Stabilise: camera-shake removal on any video clip (strength 0 to 1), measured once per file with Apple's Vision framework and applied identically in the preview and the export; the picture is cropped by 6 to 24% to make room.
- Follow: any text or graphic can move with the subject's face (found already) or with an object you draw a box around on the preview (tracked with Apple's Vision framework, kept with the file), hanging above, on or below it with a nudge; the assistant does the same with follow_target and track_object.
- Effects layer: confetti, sparks, snow, petals, rain and bokeh particles, light leaks and a lens flare you can place and keyframe (Overlays tab), glow (bloom) and colour fringing (RGB split) on any clip (Effects tab), a film-burn transition, and title cards on moving backgrounds (flow, aurora, rays, bokeh, grid). All drawn from time and a seed, so the preview and the export show the same frame.
- Sound per clip: tone presets (clear voice, podcast, warm, bright, bass boost, radio, phone, muffled, big) with bass, mids, treble and levelling on top, built from one description into ffmpeg filters for the export and Web Audio nodes for the preview; volume points over a clip's life (dip under a line, swell for the drop); beat marks on music clips with snapping to them.
- Export every format in one go (9:16, 16:9, 1:1, 4:5, re-cropped around the same focus), and save the frame at the playhead as a cover picture.
- Keyboard shortcuts on the ? key (space, frame stepping, split, undo per assistant message, markers, zoom). A Before button in the Colour section shows a clip as recorded while held.
- Timeline: markers on the ruler (M drops one at the playhead; write a note on it and the assistant reads it as a request for that moment), snapping to edges, markers and beats, b-roll on as many rows as needed, and a roll edit on every cut of the main track (drag the grip between two clips: one gives what the other takes, nothing else moves).
- Nested sequences (compound clips): group a run of clips and what sits over them into one clip; it is rendered once with the same exporter and then takes speed, grades, masks and transitions as a whole; expand it to change what is inside. Tools `group_sequence` and `expand_sequence`.
- Colour grading per clip, Lightroom style, with clarity and sharpen sliders and an Auto button that reads the picture and sets blacks, whites and exposure: exposure, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks, temperature, tint, vibrance, tone curves (RGB and per channel), HSL by hue range, and LUTs (import .cube files; they are kept in the library). Copy a grade to every clip to match them. The assistant grades from a brief too ("fix the orange cast", "matte film look", "match the clips"). Picture-in-picture for b-roll. Overlays: animated cards, labels, counters, a Reddit post card (a thread's title, votes and comment count in Reddit's shape, filled from the research tool), and a reaction GIF search (Tenor key in Settings) that drops a small looping GIF at the playhead, clear of the face.
- Sounds: a library of 100 sound effects (the social set: keyboard, phone typing, pings, shutters, coins, cash, record scratch, doorbell; the comedy set: vine boom, sad trombone, rimshot, crickets, laughs, buzzers) with search, favourites and shelves. Drop your own sound files on the tab to keep them for every project, and search Freesound for more (free key in Settings; CC0 and CC BY only, credit lines kept).
- Memes: the meme sounds, reaction GIF search, meme templates (Imgflip's public list) with top and bottom text in the meme style, and the meme text styles.
- Girlypop: a tab with everything cute, pink and girly in one place. 22 bundled script, handwriting, bubble and fairy-tale fonts (Chewy, Fredoka, Baloo 2, Parisienne, Great Vibes, Sacramento, Gochi Hand, Emilys Candy, Princess Sofia, Twinkle Star and more); caption styles bubble, sparkle (stars twinkle around the spoken word), coquette (a heart beats after it), Y2K (chrome letters) and kawaii; text styles bubblegum, pink hook, pink sticker, coquette, love note, Y2K, pink neon, Burn Book, princess, diary, cherry and twinkle; looks blush, peach, cotton candy, Barbie, vanilla, rosy film, soft glam and Y2K; hearts, sparkles, bubbles, butterflies, glitter and a pink light leak on the effects layer; heart and sparkle transitions; emoji stickers; the cute sounds; and a chick flick shelf: the best-known lines from 44 films and shows (Mean Girls, Clueless, Legally Blonde, The Devil Wears Prada, Barbie, Gossip Girl, Sex and the City, The Notebook, Pitch Perfect, Gilmore Girls and more) as quote cards or stickers, with the scene itself a GIF search away. Two recipes, Girlypop and Coquette, set the whole style in one click, and the assistant speaks it too: "make it girlypop", "coquette", "Barbie", "Y2K", or "put the Mean Girls line here".
- Music: your music or the bundled library (named by mood), voiceover in 26 natural voices (English, Hindi, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese and Chinese) or the Mac's own, and the final mix: loudness normalisation, noise reduction and voice clean-up on export.
- Export: 9:16, 16:9, 1:1 or 4:5, 720p to 4K, with the sound brought to -14 LUFS. Files go to `~/Documents/Local Studio` (change it in Settings).

**Find footage** (button on the home screen, and "Find footage online" on the Media tab). Public footage and pictures by prompt, from sources whose licences allow use in a video: Pexels and Pixabay (free to use, no credit; each needs a free key), Wikimedia Commons and Openverse (public domain and Creative Commons), the Internet Archive (old films and historical footage) and NASA (space and science), all without a key. Every result shows its licence; CC BY and CC BY-SA files keep their credit line with the file and the assistant passes it on for the caption. Pick the shots and start a project from them, or add them to the open one. The assistant has the same search (`find_footage`, `add_footage`) and can read what people are saying on Reddit (`research_reddit`, through a public archive of Reddit, since Reddit itself refuses scripts), plus web search and page reading when "allow web" is on in the Plan page profile. So "make a fun, girly video about the show's finale with a voiceover, from the Reddit drama, with public footage" is one request: it reads the threads, writes the script, finds a shot per beat, records the voice, lays the footage along it and styles it.

**Links.** Paste a link into the chat and the assistant opens it (`read_link`): a Reddit thread (post, top comments and its pictures, through the archive), a YouTube video (title, channel, description, thumbnail and the transcript when it has captions), a public Google Doc, an article page (its text and the pictures on it) or a picture file. Pictures it lists can be pulled into the project (`add_link_media`); each file keeps its source address and a "Source:" line, and what goes on screen is your call, as with anything you attach. Text copied out of a document keeps its hyperlinks when pasted into the chat box (the addresses are written after the linked words). Sites that need a login (Instagram, TikTok, X, Netflix) show a script only a title and a lead picture, and some news sites refuse scripts: the assistant says which link failed and asks you to paste the text or attach the pictures. With a Google key (below) it can also search images by prompt (`search_images`).

**Plan** (button on the home screen). Your footage library in one place: drop everything you have shot and the planner describes each clip. It asks about your account, audience, platforms, goals and capacity, saves a profile, keeps notes on what works on each platform, turns posts you like into style cards, and builds a dated content plan with a hook, storyline, footage, caption and hashtags per post. "Make it" creates the project and queues the edit, which runs when you open it. Nothing is posted anywhere by the app.

**Settings** (home screen): which Claude model the assistants use, the export folder, a Tenor key for reaction GIF search and a Freesound key for sound search.

## Free keys (optional)

A few features talk to outside services and need your own free key. Keys are saved in your Mac's Keychain (item "Local Studio"), never in a file, so a copy of the folder, a backup or a commit never carries them and the browser only learns whether a key is set; each person who installs the app makes their own. On Linux or Windows set `STUDIO_TENOR_KEY`, `STUDIO_FREESOUND_KEY`, `STUDIO_PEXELS_KEY`, `STUDIO_PIXABAY_KEY`, `STUDIO_GOOGLE_KEY` and `STUDIO_GOOGLECX_KEY` in the environment instead.

- **GIF search (Tenor)**, used by the Memes and Girlypop tabs and by the assistant's `find_gif`. Tenor keys come from Google Cloud: go to https://console.cloud.google.com/apis/library/tenor.googleapis.com, sign in, create a project (any name), press **Enable**, then open **APIs & Services → Credentials → Create credentials → API key**. Copy the key and paste it into **Settings → Tenor API key**. Restricting the key to the Tenor API (Edit key → API restrictions) is a good habit. There is no charge; Tenor's free quota is far more than an editor uses.
- **Stock footage (Pexels, Pixabay)**, used by Find footage and the assistant's `find_footage`. Both are free and instant: https://www.pexels.com/api/ (sign up, the key is shown on the API page) and https://pixabay.com/api/docs/ (sign up, the key is at the top of the docs page once you are logged in). Paste them into **Settings**. Their footage is free to use with no credit. The other footage sources need no key.
- **Image search by prompt (Google)**, used by the assistant's `search_images` ("photos of the cast", "the show's poster"). Google's search pages refuse scripts, so this goes through Google's Custom Search API with your key; 100 searches a day are free. In Google Cloud Console enable https://console.cloud.google.com/apis/library/customsearch.googleapis.com and make an API key (same steps as Tenor; one key can serve both). Then at https://programmablesearchengine.google.com create a search engine: name it anything, turn **Search the entire web** on, and in its settings turn **Image search** on; copy its **Search engine ID** (cx). Paste the key into **Settings → Google image search key** and the id into **Google search engine id**. Pictures found this way come with whatever rights their site has: the source is kept with each file for a credit, and you decide what goes on screen.
- **Sound search (Freesound)**, used by the Sounds tab and `find_sound_online`. Make an account at https://freesound.org, then at https://freesound.org/apiv2/apply create an API credential (any name and description) and paste the **Client secret / API key** into **Settings → Freesound API key**.

Everything else (speech, voices, faces, stabilisation, export) runs on your Mac with no key.

## Languages

If your videos are in more than one language, set them on the Plan page (You → Edit → Languages). Speech detection is then limited to those, and a clip detected as something else falls back to the first one. Hindi and Hinglish are a special case: with `hi` in the list, every clip is transcribed in whisper's Hindi mode, which hears the Hindi right and keeps the English in English. Put `en` first (`en, hi`) for Hinglish captions in Latin letters (Hindi passages are romanised: "ladki", "accha", "kya hua"); put `hi` first to keep Hindi passages in Devanagari. Captions render in Devanagari, Arabic and other scripts.

## Where things live

- `data/` holds projects, the footage library and the planner's files (set `STUDIO_DATA` to move it). Projects reference your files where they are; nothing is copied unless you upload through the browser.
- `library/` holds the music, sound effects and b-roll shared by every project; add your own files there.
- `models/` holds the speech models, downloaded by setup.
